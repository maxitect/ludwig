## Notes

- Content: 5 original puzzles, 6x6 to 10x10 (`first-footing` 6x6 with 15 givens, `lamp-and-lens` 7x7 with 22, `borrowed-time` 8x8 with 31, `the-long-way-round` 9x9 with 38, `full-house` 10x10 with 55). Each was cut from a seeded random area partition by dropping givens one at a time while the solution stayed unique. The generator was a throwaway script and is not committed.
- Worst `countSolutions` time over the content: 395 ms (`the-long-way-round`); the others are 3 to 33 ms. Nothing is near the 5 s line, so no givens were added after the fact.
- `check` is rule-based, like troix and norinori: it returns `{ correct, wrongParts }` where `wrongParts` are cells of areas of the wrong size, overwritten givens and empty cells, all computed from the answer alone. `checkCell` and `revealCell` are not exported. `loadSolution` solves the givens, as norinori's does, and `check` ignores it.
- The area rule was implemented in one place (`brokenIndexes`): an area is broken when it is bigger than its digit (two same-size areas that touch merge, so this covers the touching rule) or smaller and walled in. The solver uses the same function for its live marks, so a size-3 area that can still grow is not flagged.
- Migration `drizzle/20261023191225_fillomino_tables` already has a descriptive name.
- The solver is a `CellGrid` with `PuzzleKeyboard` digits and `regionBorderClasses` for the area borders (a filled cell next to an empty one also gets a rule). It does not use `DigitGrid`, which is square only, has no notes need here, and fillomino allows rows != cols.
- SPEC §7.4.4: the fillomino row now says how `loadSolution` and `check` work.
