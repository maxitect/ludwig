## Design

- `reflections/grade.ts` (`solveLayout`, `solveReflections`, `gradeReflections`). A **way** of a beam is a setting of the undecided slots along it that carries it from one lettered port to its partner. Every technique is read straight off the board; nothing is assumed and then propagated through other techniques (SPEC §4 authoring rule, research §5.2). Ladder, easiest first: **1** beam forcing (looking one undecided slot ahead along a lettered beam, from either end, a slot that every way sets the same is decided), **2** two slots on a beam (the same, two slots ahead), **3** three slots, **4** five slots, **5** blocked beam (a mirror that leaves some other lettered beam no way at all to reach its partner is ruled out, each mirror checked on the spot; same shape as the merged star battle and norinori checks). A way that runs past the look-ahead is kept as it stands, so the deductions stay sound.
- `reflections/generate.ts`: generator `reflections`, version 1, registered (generator and grader) in `src/puzzles/generators.ts`. Per candidate: sides from the difficulty (d1 4 to 5, d2 5 to 6, d3 6 to 7, d4 7 to 9, d5 8 to 10, rows and cols drawn independently); random planted mirrors; a share of them made slots (at most 16); every pair of edge ports the mirrors join lettered. Slots are fixed back to their mirror until `countSolutions === 1`. Then, to a fixpoint, letters are dropped one pair at a time and further mirrors turned into slots, each kept only while the puzzle stays unique and the techniques up to the target still solve it. Accepted when the grader's difficulty is exactly the target. At least two lettered pairs remain.
- `engine.ts`: `follow` is now exported (one word; no behaviour change) so the grader walks beams with the same code as `trace`.
- **Ticket note.** The ticket asks to "remove labels and slots while `countSolutions === 1`". Removing a slot means turning it into a fixed mirror (the planted solution keeps its mirror); the generator also does the reverse (adds slots) because dropping labels alone left almost everything at grade 1 or 2.
