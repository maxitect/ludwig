## Design

- `src/puzzles/sudoku/generate-variants.ts`: `sudoku-killer` and `sudoku-xv`, version 1, registered in `generators.ts`, seeded through `generateGraded`. Classic `sudoku` v1 is untouched (only `randomSolvedGrid` is now exported).
- Killer: random solved grid, cut into edge-connected cages of distinct digits (size limit 3 at difficulty 1-2, 5 above), sums from the grid. Givens are removed in shuffled order, then neighbouring cages merge (up to 6 cells, distinct digits), each step only while the grader, held to the target, still solves. A killer can end with no givens (it does at difficulty 1 and 2 on some seeds).
- XV: random solved grid, every adjacent pair making 10 or 5 marked, so the negative rule covers the rest. Marks are never dropped (dropping one would forbid its pair). Givens removed while the grader still solves.
- Grader (`grade-variants.ts`, merged into the ladder by `techniquesFor`): cage single (1), cage combinations (2), cage sum arithmetic with 1, 2, 3 open cells (levels 3, 4, 5: n rows, columns or boxes hold 45n, so the cells left over against the cages must make the difference), XV pair (2, each digit needs a partner among the other cell's candidates, marked or by the negative rule), and a placed-digit hook that narrows XV partners at once. `gradeSudoku` takes an optional `Variant`; classic, jigsaw and rainbow grades are unchanged. `puzzles:grade` now grades killer and XV files.
- A generated killer or XV puzzle only reaches `countSolutions` once its grade equals the target (the count dominated generation time at difficulty 5).
- `scripts/format-content.ts`: nested arrays inside arrays now print (not needed after all by the output, harmless).
- No research conflict worth noting; the ticket's "cage combination tables" are the `cage combinations` technique.
